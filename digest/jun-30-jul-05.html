India-Japan Summit Rewires the Partnership

June 30 – July 5, 2026
The Week in One Paragraph

The India-Japan summit delivered 16 agreements spanning defence co-development, AI, economic security, and energy resilience — the most consequential bilateral outcome of the year so far. At home, VB-G RAM G replaced MGNREGA on July 1, restructuring India's rural employment guarantee for tens of millions of workers. Delhi notified its EV Policy 2026 mandating electric-only commercial transport from 2027, the Finance Ministry cleared Rs 1.25 lakh crore for Semiconductor Mission 2.0, and PFC-REC approved a merger to create an Rs 11 lakh crore power entity. Fuel prices fell as oil dipped below $70 for the first time since the Iran war. In the background, Iran buried Supreme Leader Khamenei as ships continued avoiding the disputed Hormuz route.

Deep Dive

India-Japan Summit Rewires the Partnership

What Happened

Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi's maiden India visit (July 1–3) produced 16 signed agreements at the 16th Annual Summit — the most expansive set of outcomes in the partnership's history. Three landmark documents set the agenda: a Joint Declaration on Economic Security, a Joint Statement on AI, and a Joint Statement on Energy Resilience. The centrepiece was the Naval Radio Antenna "Unicorn" agreement — the first-ever India-Japan defence co-development project. The summit had been building since April, when the two countries held their first AI Strategic Dialogue, and accelerated through May-June with discussions on supply-chain deals and Indo-Pacific alignment.

Why It Matters

Defence co-development

The Unicorn project moves India-Japan from defence dialogues and exercises to jointly building military technology. Japan has historically been cautious about defence technology transfers, and Takaichi's review of Japan's Three Principles on Transfer of Defence Equipment signals a broader opening.

Economic security

The roadmap targets semiconductors, quantum technologies, critical minerals, and advanced manufacturing — sectors where both countries are reducing dependence on China. Over $10 billion in new Japanese investment is expected from 120+ business agreements signed in the past year, with a 10-year target of 10 trillion yen.

AI partnership

Indian institutions including IIT Bombay, SarvamAI, and BharatGen signed agreements with Japanese counterparts to co-develop next-generation large language models — combining India's software scale with Japan's precision engineering.

Energy resilience

Japan will assist India in building strategic oil reserves and establishing 1,000 biogas plants. Both countries' joint statement explicitly referenced maintaining unimpeded navigation through the Strait of Hormuz — a pointed message amid the ongoing Iran conflict.

What's Next

Modi's upcoming three-nation visit to Indonesia, Australia, and New Zealand will extend the Indo-Pacific framework. Watch the Unicorn antenna project for a signal on how fast defence co-development can actually move.

Bottom Line: India-Japan has crossed from strategic alignment to strategic integration — co-building defence technology, co-developing AI, and jointly hedging against energy and supply-chain shocks.
Five More Stories That Matter
Story 1

Delhi's EV Policy 2026: Electric-Only Autos by 2027

Delhi notified its most aggressive transport electrification mandate yet. From January 2027, only electric three-wheelers and light goods carriers can be newly registered. From April 2028, new petrol/CNG two-wheeler registrations end. The policy includes scrappage incentives of up to Rs 1 lakh for BS-IV and older vehicles, runs until March 2030, and mandates schools to convert 10% of their fleet to EVs within two years. The draft had been in consultation since April — this is now a regulatory mandate with hard deadlines.

Story 2

PFC-REC Merger Creates Rs 11 Lakh Crore Power Lender

Power Finance Corporation and Rural Electrification Corporation approved a merger scheme with a share exchange ratio of 88 PFC shares for every 100 REC shares. The combined entity would be India's largest power-sector lender with an Rs 11 lakh crore loan book. First discussed in May and delayed by war-related fiscal pressures, the merger was formally approved on June 30 and now awaits Presidential clearance.

Story 3

Rs 1.25 Lakh Crore Cleared for Semiconductor Mission 2.0

The Finance Ministry's Expenditure Finance Committee cleared the second phase of India's semiconductor push, paving the way for Cabinet approval. ISM 2.0 will focus on R&D, chip design, and advanced manufacturing nodes — moving beyond phase one's assembly and packaging emphasis. Two new semiconductor units were already approved in Gujarat in May. India accounts for nearly 20% of the world's semiconductor design engineers but has no indigenous design firms — the gap this phase aims to close.

Story 4

MGNREGA Replaced: VB-G RAM G Takes Effect July 1

India's two-decade-old rural employment guarantee was formally replaced on July 1 by the Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025. The new law raises guaranteed workdays from 100 to 125 per year and introduces a Rs 300/day national wage floor — lifting wages in 21 states that were below that threshold. But the biggest structural change is fiscal: VB-G RAM G shifts from MGNREGA's 100% Centre-funded wage bill to a 60:40 Centre-State cost-sharing model (90:10 for northeastern and Himalayan states). The Rs 95,692 crore budget allocation for FY27 signals scale, but several states have already raised concerns about the additional fiscal burden.

Story 5

Karnataka Gig Workers Act Survives First Court Challenge

Swiggy, Zomato, Zepto, and IAMAI challenged the constitutional validity of Karnataka's Gig Workers Act in the High Court, arguing it conflicts with the Centre's Social Security Code. The court refused to grant an interim stay and ordered platforms to deposit welfare fund fees with the court registry, though it also said no coercive action can be taken until the next hearing on August 14. This is the most significant constitutional challenge against any state-level gig worker law to date — and the fact that the court did not stay the Act will embolden other states considering similar legislation. Rajasthan, Bihar, and Telangana already have their own gig worker welfare laws in place.

Sector Spotlight: Trade

Trade moved in two opposing directions this week. India accelerated bilateral deal-making: US Ambassador Sergio Gor said the India-US trade pact is "in the last 1 or 2%." India and Canada are pushing to conclude their deal by year-end. The India-Japan summit opened new channels in semiconductors, critical minerals, and advanced manufacturing. But the West Asia crisis has stalled FTA talks with Israel and the GCC bloc, and the US declined to renew the USMCA pact with Mexico and Canada. The government extended customs duty waivers on 40 petrochemical products until July 15. Trade policy is now on two tracks: accelerating deals with aligned partners while managing disruptions from geopolitical conflict.

On Our Radar

WhatsApp username controversy. The government asked WhatsApp to pause its username rollout, then sent notices to Telegram and Signal over the same feature — citing fraud and impersonation risks. Zoho's Arattai dropped its username feature in response. A new regulatory posture: the government asserting platform-design authority, not just content-moderation authority.

Iran: Khamenei buried, Hormuz still contested. India sent Bihar Governor and an MoS to represent the country at Khamenei's funeral. Trump says US-Iran talks will take place in Qatar, but ships continue avoiding the disputed route.

New Army Chief's VIJAY roadmap. General Dhiraj Seth took over as India's 31st Army Chief with a force modernisation vision. Air Marshal Tejinder Singh became Chief of IDS to COSC.

Fuel prices fall as oil drops below $70. OMCs cut commercial LPG by Rs 183.50 per cylinder and slashed jet fuel prices. Private player Nayara cut petrol by Rs 5/litre and diesel by Rs 3/litre. Watch whether PSU oil companies pass through further cuts or rebuild margins.

Cabinet doubles NIIF commitment to Rs 60,000 crore. An additional Rs 30,000 crore was approved for the National Investment and Infrastructure Fund on June 29, funding NIIF Infrastructure Fund II with a target corpus of Rs 30,000 crore across transport, energy, digital infrastructure, and e-mobility.

DRDO gets enhanced financial powers. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh released DFP-2026, granting DRDO greater financial autonomy for R&D projects — including dedicated funding for trial campaigns and pre-project research. Aimed at faster development and induction of indigenous defence technologies.

TMC continues to fracture. Former Bengal minister Chandrima Bhattacharya — a key Mamata loyalist — quit all TMC posts, the latest in the party's accelerating fragmentation since early June.
